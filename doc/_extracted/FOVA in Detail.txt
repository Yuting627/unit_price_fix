KS parameters\nThe decision making whether a store is considered to have questionable data in regards to it’s structure  is mainly driven by the  comparisons of the frequency distributions of items according to:\nNumber of units sold\nStructure of the item code\nPrice\nThe comparisons are done by means of a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test.\nThe KS-statistic in itself appears to be a good measure for structural changes in the data files. However, it seems that the textbook critical values for the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test are not very well adapted to our particular application. \nPart of the problem stems from the fact that some of the assumptions of the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test are not respected/appropriate in our situation:\nOur ‘samples’ are not independent The KS critical values are however assuming both samples are independent. \nespecially for the test on units-sold the null-hypothesis of the KS-test is not appropriate. The null-hypothesis of the classical KS-test is that the two distributions are equal (it means that the KS-test on units sold is detecting all cases where there is some trend between the two weeks) As we only want to consider a store questionable when the observed trend is very extreme thus we should be looking to reject a H0: ‘data change within reasonable limits’. \nIt is because this inappropriateness of the traditional null-hypothesis for the unit sold test that the textbook critical values are much too restrictive. \nDetermination of critical values for the KS-test:\nThe issue of the proper level of the critical values can be handled by falling back on the empirical distribution. \nWe follow the same logic as the traditional hypothesis test but we may attempt to tune the model to make it more appropriate for our situation. \nIn the traditional test the critical values are determined as percentiles on the theoretical distribution of D under the null-hypothesis of no-change. \nRather then picking percentiles from the theoretical distribution, we could refer to the empirical distribution of the D’s we have observed historically in our sample.\n We could then consider only the distribution over the cases which we have not considered to be truly problematic (our new H0). \nCritical values will be selected as percentiles from this empirical distribution. In other words, we determine critical values according to what appears to be exceptional in our cumulated experience. \nStage A:\nAs the distribution of D in function of N does decrease monotonically, but only until a certain value of N is reached, after which the curve is flatter than one would expect according to theory ( In some cases the curve is even increasing) \nKS-test for price\nThe problem with the dependence of %Q (or D) on the size of the store (N) is lying in the formulation of the normalization factor F. \nFirst, the monotonous descending pattern is only present upto a certain threshold on store size.
